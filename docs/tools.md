- **`/tools/crypto-calendar`** (Crypto Calendar; built 2026-10-14): the monthly "Crypto + Market Calendar" posts of the Telegram group **@layergg** as a full-width month calendar. Source/code: `app/services/crypto_calendar_service.py` (parser + HTML), `state/calendar_state.py`, `tools_page.py::crypto_calendar_content`, styles `.cal-*` in `assets/styles.css`, tests `tests/test_crypto_calendar.py`, one-off `scripts/backfill_layergg_calendar.py`. **Data:** no new table: the posts are `news_articles` rows (source_type `telegram`, source_name `Layergg...`, title with "Calendar"/"캘린더", not "Weekly") stored by the Telegram news pipeline; the page parses their *text* on load (cached 5 min), so a new month's post appears by itself once the listener stores it. The image attached to each post holds more events than the text (about 80 vs 16-24); only the text is used. **Parser:** one "noteworthy events" line per event in three formats seen so far (`10-03 - $HYPE - text`, `Sep 1: US - text`, Korean `8월 1일: $CRCL(@circle) - text`), then an undated TBD/TBA list; intro bullets, other-month dates and everything after the footer ("See detail", disclaimer, hashtags) are ignored; 🔥 marks important events. Each event gets a colour like the source image: **crypto** green, **macro** orange (country/central-bank/agency names, CPI/FOMC/GDP/bond words), **stocks & earnings** blue (earnings words and listed companies such as NVDA/HOOD). **UI:** month title with `‹ ›` arrows, month chips from January 2026 to the current month (dimmed when that month has no post; it opens on the latest month that has events), colour legend, then a Monday-Friday week grid across the full width (empty cells for days outside the month, today outlined; event cards with a CoinMarketCap logo on crypto tickers, bold ticker, text), a "Weekend events" list (cards with their date), a "Date to be announced" list and a link to that month's post on t.me. Below 768px the grid becomes a day-by-day list (empty days hidden). Event text is HTML-escaped. **Which posts count:** every monthly post starts with the 🗓 emoji, is titled "... Calendar" (or Korean "캘린더"), ends with `#Calendar #Schedule` and "See detail @Layergg" and carries an image; the title wording varies ("2026 Crypto January Calendar", "2026 Market May Calendar", "2026 June Crypto + Market Calendar"), so nothing requires the words "Crypto" or "Market", and the service SQL is just `title ~ calendar|캘린더` minus "weekly" (the 🤡 "Weekly Crypto Calendar Highlight" posts are not parsed yet). A month's post is often published the day before (the January 2026 one on 31 Dec 2025), so the month and year come from the title, not the post date. **Year:** the page shows the latest year that has a parsed post (not a fixed 2026): 2026 stays until the first 2027 post exists (even published on 31 Dec), then only 2027 is shown, from January (`view_year`, `month_keys`); chips run from January to the current month, or to a later month whose post is already out. **Live refresh:** while the tab is open `CalendarState.watch` re-checks every 60 s (server cache 45 s): a new or edited post updates the chips, and if the visitor is on the newest month with events ("following") the new month becomes the active tab and is shown by itself; a visitor reading an older month stays on it and only gets the new chip. Verified by inserting temporary November and December posts into the local database with the page open (no reload, about 50 s each), then deleting them. **Coverage (local and live):** Jan-Oct 2026, all ten months (Jan 13 events, Feb 24, Mar 17, Apr 25, May 15, Jun 20, Jul 16, Aug 15 + 3 TBA, Sep 14 + 2, Oct 16 + 8; a 2025 December post is stored but not shown). Everything before August was older than the news pipeline's 30-day window, so it was fetched once with `python -m scripts.backfill_layergg_calendar [YYYY-MM-DD]` (default 2025-12-01; read-only, through the pipeline's own `store_message`; stop the Telegram listener first, restart it after) and copied to the live database. The first version of that script required "Crypto" in the title and started on 1 Jan, which is why January and May were missed. The card on `/tools` and the dropdown entry link here.
- **`/tools/cpi-fomc`** (shown as **Forex Factory** in the Tools dropdown, the `/tools` card and the page heading "Forex Factory Calendar" since 2026-10-15; the URL kept its old slug; built 2026-10-15): this week's US inflation (CPI), **producer prices (PPI, added 2026-10-16)**, Federal Reserve (FOMC) and **Non-Farm Employment Change (NFP, added 2026-10-15)** events with exact date and time, impact, actual / forecast / previous, updating live. Code: `app/services/macro_calendar_service.py`, `state/macro_calendar_state.py`, `tools_page.py::cpi_fomc_content`, styles `.mc-*`, tests `tests/test_macro_calendar.py`. **Sources (all free, no trial):** (1) *schedule, forecast, previous*: the public Forex Factory calendar feed `https://nfs.faireconomy.media/ff_calendar_thisweek.json` (no key; fields title, currency, ISO date with the New York offset, impact, forecast, previous; **no actual or revised values**; this week only, the next-week file does not exist). Forex Factory itself has no API and blocks scripted page requests, so its pages are never read. The feed is **rate limited** (HTTP 429 "Rate Limited", `Retry-After` about 5 minutes after a handful of requests: it hit the dev machine while testing), so it is fetched at most once every 3 minutes for all viewers, a `Retry-After` is obeyed, and the last good copy is kept in `logs/macro_feed.json` (survives restarts and blocks; shown as "last copy" for up to 6 h). (2) *actual*: the official US sources, looked up on a background thread (never blocking the page) only after a release and only for events still empty, at most every 2 minutes and 20 times a day: **CPI from the BLS public API** (v1, no key; one POST for the four CPI series; `CPI m/m` and `Core CPI m/m` from the seasonally adjusted index, `y/y` from the not adjusted one, one decimal, for the month before the release; months BLS lists as "-" are skipped) and the **Federal Funds Rate** decision from FRED's public CSV of the target's upper bound (FRED can lag a day; it stalls browser-style User-Agents, so that request sends the HTTP library's default). Found actuals are kept in `logs/macro_actuals.json`. September 2026 CPI (released 14 Oct) was filled in 25 s after the feed came back: m/m 0.6%, y/y 3.7%, core m/m 0.3%, core y/y 2.6%. (3) *optional extra*: the Apify actor `scrapemint/forexfactory-economic-calendar` (pay per event, $0.01 per row, the first 25 rows of every run free) when `APIFY_TOKEN` is set in `.env`: a run asks for USD, high and medium impact, this week (under 25 rows, so normally free), and only for released events the free sources could not fill, at most once per 3 minutes and `MACRO_ACTUALS_MAX_RUNS_PER_DAY` (40) a day. **Not verified against the real actor** (no token was available): the row fields are read tolerantly (title/event, currency, actual, revised), and any failure is logged and ignored. **What is shown:** only events whose title has `CPI`, `FOMC`, `Federal Funds Rate`, `Fed Chair` or `Non-Farm Employment Change` (so the FOMC members' speeches, the rate decision, the statement/minutes/press conference, the Fed Chair, and the monthly jobs report; green NFP chip). The NFP actual ("29K") is the month's change in BLS total nonfarm payrolls (series CES0000000001, thousands, this month's level minus the revised previous month's), filled in like CPI after the release. **Optional BLS key:** `BLS_API_KEY` in `.env` (free registration at https://data.bls.gov/registrationEngine/, a key emailed and activated by link) switches the BLS request from v1 (25 requests a day) to v2 (500 a day, `registrationkey` in the POST); without it everything still works. "USD only" (default) or "All currencies" (e.g. China, eurozone CPI). Times are Kuala Lumpur with the New York time under each, grouped by KL day, past rows dimmed, the next 30 minutes highlighted, a "NEXT EVENT" card with a countdown (the **nearest upcoming event of any kind**, a Fed Chair or member speech included; changed 2026-10-15), animated dots while an actual is expected, a neutral ▲/▼ when the actual is above/below the forecast, and a clear message (not "no events") while the feed is blocked and no copy exists. **One month ahead (2026-10-15):** the page shows today (Kuala Lumpur date) and the next 30 days; earlier days' tables are removed. The free feed only covers the current week, so `macro_calendar_service.get_schedule` adds: every feed event is **remembered** in `logs/macro_events.json` (40 days, so the month list has the past days; a changed time replaces the old row); **FOMC meetings** from the Federal Reserve's own calendar page (no key; decision day 14:00 New York, parsed by `parse_fomc_page`); **CPI and jobs-report dates** from FRED's release calendar (`fred_api_key`, `FRED_API_KEY` in `.env`, free at https://fredaccount.stlouisfed.org/apikeys; BLS's schedule pages return 403 to scripts) as "scheduled" placeholder rows (8:30 New York) that the feed's real rows (forecast, previous) replace once that week is published. Without the FRED key a CPI or jobs report is only known from the week it is in the feed. The release schedules are cached 12 h in `logs/macro_schedule.json` (a failed fetch is retried after 30 min). **Telegram** (`app/services/macro_reminder_service.py`, tests `tests/test_macro_reminders.py`): reminders 7 days, 3 days and 1 hour before each CPI release, NFP report, FOMC event and Fed Chair speech (single FOMC members' speeches are only in the list), sent once each (`logs/macro_reminders_sent.json`), live server only (`TELEGRAM_DIGEST`); an event first seen late gets one message for the tightest offset reached. `/forex_factory` (also `/forex-factory`) lists the current calendar month's events by day, past ones with ✅ and the actual. `MacroCalendarState.watch` re-reads every 30 s while the tab is open (verified: it recovered from a rate-limit block by itself, without a reload). Phones get a labelled card per event.

## Features

<details><summary>Fear & Greed Index</summary>

A semicircle gauge (server-built SVG, 5 colour zones), the value and label in the zone's colour, and Yesterday / Last week / Last month values. **Sources** (chosen in the "Data source" popup): Alternative.me (free, no key, default) or CoinMarketCap (`COINMARKETCAP_API_KEY`, 1-2 credits per refresh). Cached 15 minutes in-process.
</details>

<details><summary>Altcoin / Bitcoin Season</summary>

Index 0-100 = share of the top 50 coins (stablecoins, wrapped/staked/bridged tokens excluded) that beat Bitcoin over the window; 75+ = Altcoin Season, 25 or less = Bitcoin Season, otherwise Neutral. Shows a marker bar, "N of 50 beat Bitcoin", and the 5 best performers. **Sources**: CoinMarketCap 90 days (standard method, needs `COINMARKETCAP_API_KEY`, 1 credit per refresh) or CoinGecko 30 days (free, no key; CoinGecko's free tier has no 90-day figure, so it reacts faster). Cached 30 minutes. blockchaincenter.net was not used: it offers no public chart widget or data API (only a share-card image of the gauge), and embedding their whole page was tried and removed (their header/ads came with it; cropping it to just the chart would be fragile). CoinMarketCap, Coinglass and Alternative.me block iframes.
</details>

<details><summary>BTC / ETH Rainbow Chart</summary>

Drawn server-side as SVG from daily USD prices: **CoinMetrics' free community API** (BTC from 18 Jul 2010, ETH from Aug 2015; no key), with Yahoo Finance (BTC from 2014) as fallback; cached 6 hours. **Bitcoin** uses the widely used logarithmic-growth curve as the centre, `log10(price) = 2.66167 * ln(days since 2009-01-09) - 17.9183`, with nine bands spanning +/-0.6 decades around it (this is what Blockchaincenter/Coinglass-style charts use; an earlier version fitted its own regression on 2014+ data with wider bands and so looked different, with BTC reading "Still cheap" instead of "Fire sale"). **Ethereum** has no standard curve, so its centre is a least-squares fit of its own history and its bands span the historical envelope around that fit. The bands are extended about 1.5 years ahead; the header shows the current price and band; BTC/ETH tabs. Labels and colours follow the common rainbow charts (Fire sale ... Maximum bubble territory).
</details>

<details><summary>Rainbow chart: live price and hover tooltip (2026-10-13)</summary>

**Live price.** The history (CoinMetrics daily closes) only goes up to yesterday's close, so the headline price used to be that close (cached 6h). Now `market_tools_service._live_spot` reads the spot USD price from free public exchange tickers, no key: Coinbase, then Kraken, then Binance (USDT); cached 10s. `ToolsState.rainbow_live_loop` re-reads it every 15s while the page is open and moves the price (through `_fmt_usd`), the band badge, a "Live · <time> UTC · <source>" line with a pulsing dot, and the chart's last segment + live dot. The chart itself (`rainbow_svg`, cached 6h) is not re-sent: the live part is a tiny overlay SVG with the same viewBox stacked on top (`rainbow_live()` -> `rainbow_overlay`, `.rainbow-overlay`). If every ticker fails, it falls back to the last daily close ("as of <date> (daily close)").

**Hover / tap tooltip** (free, our own code, no library or service): `assets/rainbow_hover.js` reads the chart model sent once per asset in `data-rainbow` on `.rainbow-chart` (geometry, band model `slope`/`icpt`/`lo`/`step`, and every daily close). On hover (or tap on touch screens) it draws a vertical guide, a dot on the price line, and a card with the date, that day's price, and all nine bands' price levels at that date with the price's band highlighted. Future dates show "Projected bands". Prices in the card mirror `_fmt_usd`; band levels are compact ($46.1K) like the axis. Touch: the card stays until a tap outside the chart; vertical scrolling still works (`touch-action: pan-y`). Theme tokens only (`.rainbow-tip` in styles.css).
</details>

<details><summary>CoinMarketCap fallback for Fear & Greed and Season (2026-10-13)</summary>

When the chosen source is CoinMarketCap and it fails (rate limit / monthly credits used up / key rejected / unreachable), the card shows the free source's result instead (Alternative.me for Fear & Greed, CoinGecko 30 days for Season) with an amber note "CoinMarketCap unavailable (<reason>), showing <source> instead." A failed CMC source is not retried for 10 minutes (`_with_cmc_fallback`, `_CMC_FAIL_TTL`).
</details>

<details><summary>TradingView embeds: DXY, total market cap, BTC dominance, ETH/BTC, TOTAL2</summary>

Free `widgetembed` iframes (same mechanism as the coin page chart), daily candles by default, KL timezone, light/dark via `rx.color_mode_cond`. Each chart has independent 1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, D, and W controls. Symbols: `CAPITALCOM:DXY`, `CRYPTOCAP:TOTAL`, `CRYPTOCAP:BTC.D`, `BINANCE:ETHBTC`, `CRYPTOCAP:TOTAL2`. **`TVC:DXY` (the usual DXY symbol) is blocked in embeds** ("only available on TradingView"), so DXY uses Capital.com's index, which tracks the same value. No key needed.
</details>

<details><summary>Data source popup and API keys</summary>

Fear & Greed and Season each have a "Data source" button that opens a popup listing the sources with a badge: "No key needed", "Key found in .env", or "Key missing". A source with a key shows the exact line to add (`NAME=your_key` in the project-root `.env`). **"Re-check .env"** re-reads the file, so a newly added key works without restarting the app. The popup opens by itself, and the widget shows an "API key needed" card, when the viewer's chosen source has no key. The choice is saved per browser (localStorage `repace_tools_fng_source` / `repace_tools_season_source`); with no choice the first usable source is used. `.env` is trusted over the process environment when the file exists (the app loads it at startup, so the environment would keep reporting a removed key).
</details>

## PNL Calculator (was Profit Calculator) and Currency Converter (2026-10-19, deployed 2026-10-19)

Two new entries in the Tools list (the `/tools` cards, the desktop dropdown and the phone/tablet submenu all come from `TOOL_PAGES`). Code: `components/calculator_pages.py`, `state/calc_state.py`, `assets/calculators.js`, `.calc-*` in `styles.css`, tests `tests/test_calculators.py` (the maths is run with node, so the tests skip without it).
- **How it works:** the arithmetic runs in the browser (`calculators.js`), so every key press answers at once, with no server round trip. The only server part is the live rate: `CalcState.load_rate` (on_load of both pages) reads `fx_service.get_rate("MYR")` and then again every 30 s while the tab is open; it reaches the script as `data-rate` on the page's `.calc-root`, and a changed rate recalculates the result by itself. **Only USD and MYR.**
- **The rate is real time:** the same live source as the Portfolio page's MYR switch (Yahoo Finance polled every 60 s, plus ExchangeRate-API and the European Central Bank as references; a quote more than 2% from the others is dropped; the freshest agreeing quote wins). The line under each tool says "Live mid-market rate · <source> · updated <time> (KL)". At weekends the quote is the last market quote and says so ("quote of 18 Oct 23:47 (KL)"). No rate at all shows a message and no result.
- **/tools/pnl-calculator** (renamed from Profit Calculator on 2026-10-20: the page, the menu entry and the URL; the old `/tools/profit-calculator` link redirects here): pick the currency (**USD by default**, or MYR), type the capital, choose **Profit +** or **Loss -**, type the percentage. Result: the final amount (big), the profit/loss amount and %, the formula (`$1,000.00 + 50% = $1,500.00`), and the same result in the other currency at the live rate. The capital is NOT converted when you switch currency: it just says which currency you typed (RM4,000 or $1,000). A loss over 100% is counted as 100% (result 0) with a note; a gain can be over 100%. Empty or invalid numbers show "-".
- **"Calculate from" on the PNL Calculator's Your trade card (2026-10-20):** a Percentage / Balance after trade switch under Capital. *Percentage* is the original mode. *Balance after trade* hides the profit/loss switch and the percentage and shows a **Balance after the trade** box instead: capital $1,000 and $850 left gives a big red `-$150.00`, `Loss (-15%)` and `$1,000.00 → $850.00 = -$150.00 (-15%)`; $1,200 gives a green `+$200.00`, `Profit (+20%)`; equal gives `$0.00` and no profit or loss; 0 left is a 100% loss. The other-currency line shows the change and both balances at the live rate. Pure function `tradeResult()` in `calculators.js`, test in `tests/test_calculators.py`.
- **/tools/currency-converter** (like Wise's USD to MYR page): an amount (default 1000), a USD / MYR "From" switch, a swap button, the converted amount (big) with `$1,000.00 = RM4,079.00`, both rates (`1 USD = 4.0790 MYR · 1 MYR = 0.2452 USD`) and a "Quick conversions" table (1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 500, 1,000, 5,000, 10,000) that follows the direction. A note says it is the mid-market rate (no bank margin or fee).
- **Add / subtract amounts (2026-10-20, second card, sits under the converter card in the same left column at the same width, the Quick conversions table on the right; on a phone: converter, Add / subtract, Quick conversions):** `$1,000 + $1,000 - $250 = $1,750.00`. One **Currency** USD / MYR switch (no per-row currency) sets the symbol of every box; the amounts sit side by side in one row (a third wraps to the next row) with a **+ / -** switch between each pair (worked left to right, so a total can go negative); starts with $1,000 + $1,000; "+ Add amount" (top right) adds rows up to 10, the x removes one (never below 2); an empty row is skipped with its operator, non-numeric text shows "Check the amounts". The first amount is the **main value**: next to the big total a green / red **percentage** shows the rise or drop from it (`$1,000 - $250 = $750 (-25%)`, `$1,000 + $1,000 = $2,000 (+100%)`; none when the main value is 0). The Currency switch is full width, each tab 50%; every amount box has the same width (on a phone the + / - switch sits centred between two stacked full-width boxes). Shows the big total, the formula, the total converted to the other currency at the live rate (`≈ RM8,163.00`) and both rates. Code: `calculator_pages.py::_sum_card`, kind `sum` + pure `sumTerms()` in `calculators.js`, tests in `tests/test_calculators.py`.
- Checked in a browser: $1,000 + 50% = $1,500.00, loss 50% = $500.00, RM4,000 + 50% = RM6,000.00 (= $1,470.95 at 4.079), loss 150% capped; converter 1,000 USD = RM4,079.00, swap, 250.5 MYR = $61.41; both entries in the desktop dropdown, the phone submenu and the `/tools` cards; 1440 / 834 / 375 px, dark and light, no horizontal scroll, no console errors.

**Telegram versions (2026-10-19):** `/currency-converter $1000` converts dollars to ringgit and `/currency-converter RM1000` ringgit to dollars; `/pnl-calculator $1000 + 50%` (profit) and `/pnl-calculator $1000 - 50%` (loss), with RM accepted too (`/pnl-calculator RM4000 - 25%`: the capital's currency is the result's, plus the other currency at the live rate). Menu names use underscores (`/currency_converter`, `/pnl_calculator`); the hyphen spelling typed by hand works. The braces in the usage templates are tolerated (`{RM1000}`, `${$1000} ${+} ${50%}`), a number without a symbol counts as USD, `+50%` may be written without spaces, and a bad input gets a one-line usage. Same maths and rate as the website (`app/services/calc_service.py`; connected chats only, like the other data commands). Listed in the bot menu and `/view_all_command`. Tests `tests/test_telegram_calculators.py`.

## Capital Splitter (2026-10-19, deployed 2026-10-19)

`/tools/capital-splitter`, the next entry in the Tools list (cards, desktop dropdown and phone submenu come from `TOOL_PAGES`): split a capital into several parts. Same code family as the calculators (`calculator_pages.py::capital_splitter_content`, `assets/calculators.js` kind `split`, `.calc-*` styles, tests in `tests/test_calculators.py`); the maths runs in the browser.
- **Inputs:** currency (USD default, or MYR), capital (default 1000), **Equal parts** or **Custom %**, number of parts (2-12 with - / +). Quick splits (smaller number first): Equal 2, 20 / 20 / 60, 30 / 30 / 40, 20 / 30 / 50, 25 x 4, 10 / 20 / 30 / 40 (a preset switches to Custom % with that many parts); **Make equal** returns to the equal split.
- **Table:** one row per part with its percent (typed in Custom mode), its amount, and the same amount in the other currency at the live rate (the "In MYR / In USD" column; hidden under 520 px so the table never scrolls on a phone), a Total row, and a status line: "Total 100% ✓" (green), "Total 95% · 5% left to assign ($50.00)" (amber) or "Total 170% · over by 70%" (red). Percentages that do not add to 100 are never scaled: each part is just its % of the capital and the line says what is left or over.
- **Cents add up:** amounts are shared out in whole cents (largest remainder), so $1,000 in 3 equal parts is $333.34 + $333.33 + $333.33 (never $999.99), and 20 / 20 / 60 of $1,000 is $200 + $200 + $600. Typing in a percentage box never rebuilds the rows, so the cursor stays put.
- Checked in a browser (dark; light checked on the other calculators): default 3 parts of $1,000, stepper (min 2), the 20 / 20 / 60 preset, typing 30 -> total 110% warning, over/under messages, Make equal, MYR with RM4,000, 375 px with no horizontal scroll and no console errors.
